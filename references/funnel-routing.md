# Funnel routing

Use this reference to identify the earliest material break rather than optimizing the most visible symptom.

## Funnel spine

```text
Qualified traffic
→ Landing-page comprehension and intent
→ Signup or trial start
→ First experienced value / activation
→ Upgrade motivation and purchase
→ Retention and expansion
```

The user may use different event names. Preserve their actual product journey while mapping it to this spine.

## Routing logic

### 1. Check traffic quality first

Traffic is a probable primary bottleneck when several of these align:

- acquisition sources attract users outside the target segment;
- one channel has low cost but materially worse activation and paid conversion;
- ad or content promise does not match the landing page or product;
- branded, referral, or high-intent traffic converts normally while broad traffic collapses;
- signups contain obvious low-intent, irrelevant, or non-buyer cohorts.

Do not infer poor traffic solely from a high blended bounce rate.

### 2. Check landing-page conversion

Landing-page conversion is a probable primary bottleneck when traffic intent is reasonably supported but:

- users cannot explain the product after five seconds;
- qualified traffic leaves before meaningful engagement;
- message, proof, CTA, or signup friction blocks the next step;
- conversion is weak across otherwise high-intent channels.

### 3. Check activation

Activation is a probable primary bottleneck when signups arrive but few users experience core value, time to value is long, or the largest drop occurs before the Aha Moment.

Do not optimize payment pressure before users reliably experience value.

### 4. Check trial-to-paid and pricing

Trial-to-paid is a probable primary bottleneck when users activate and use the product, but do not develop upgrade motivation, encounter poor upgrade timing, cannot understand packaging, or abandon checkout.

Distinguish:

- value not experienced → activation problem;
- value experienced but no reason to upgrade → packaging or paid-motivation problem;
- desire to buy but cannot choose → pricing-page clarity problem;
- plan selected but payment abandoned → checkout or trust problem.

### 5. Check retention and expansion

Retention is the primary bottleneck when users activate and some pay, but durable usage or revenue does not persist. Examine cohort retention, customer and revenue churn, habit loops, repeated value, cancellation reasons, downgrades, and expansion paths.

Do not label a product retained because aggregate active users grow while individual cohorts decay.

## Choosing the primary bottleneck

Prefer the earliest stage where:

1. the eligible sample and metric definition are credible;
2. performance is materially weak relative to the product's own stronger segments, historical baseline, or appropriate context;
3. the weakness can plausibly explain downstream loss;
4. a focused experiment can validate the cause.

When every stage is uncertain, do not choose based on confidence theater. State a provisional route and recommend the smallest instrumentation or research action that will resolve it.

## Common misroutes

- More traffic when current traffic does not activate.
- A homepage redesign when ad-to-page message mismatch is the dominant issue.
- More upgrade prompts when users have not reached the Aha Moment.
- Discounts when users do not understand the paid value.
- Retention notifications when the product does not deliver repeated value.
- Comparing a sales-led enterprise funnel with a low-price self-serve benchmark.
