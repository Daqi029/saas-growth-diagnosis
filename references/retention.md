# Retention and expansion diagnosis

Use this reference only after acquisition, conversion, and activation are sufficiently understood. Retention reflects repeated value; it is not repaired by adding reminders when the underlying job is not recurring or valuable.

## Start with the right retention question

Clarify:

- retained as active, paying, or both;
- customer retention versus revenue retention;
- cohort age and observation window;
- expected natural usage frequency;
- self-serve versus sales-assisted customers;
- logo churn, contraction, expansion, and reactivation.

A monthly workflow should not be judged by daily activity. Define success around the product's natural value cadence.

## Diagnose three layers

### Repeated value

- Does the customer's underlying job recur?
- Does each return visit produce a meaningful outcome?
- Does the product become more useful as data, workflows, history, or collaboration accumulates?
- Are users leaving because the job is complete rather than because the product failed?

### Habit and operational embedding

- What event predicts the next successful return?
- Is the product connected to an existing workflow or dependent system?
- Are reminders tied to unfinished value or merely generic notifications?
- Can another user, team, integration, or artifact create a legitimate return loop?

### Commercial retention and expansion

- Which cohorts, plans, industries, or acquisition channels churn most?
- Is revenue loss caused by cancellations, contraction, failed payments, or low initial fit?
- Is expansion tied to growing value such as seats, usage, workflows, or business outcomes?
- Does a pricing change alter customer mix and churn?

## Evidence to request

- cohort retention curves at a product-appropriate cadence;
- customer churn and revenue churn with consistent denominators;
- NRR components: starting revenue, expansion, contraction, and churn;
- cancellation reasons and post-cancellation interviews;
- retained versus churned behavior before loss;
- retention by channel, plan, customer segment, and activation status;
- failed-payment and involuntary-churn share.

## Common errors

- using aggregate active users instead of cohorts;
- blending newly acquired and mature customers;
- treating activation and retention as unrelated;
- calling all non-returning users “churned” before the natural return window;
- pushing notifications without a valuable reason to return;
- reducing price before identifying whether the issue is fit, value, service, or payment failure.

## Candidate experiments

- Strengthen one repeated-value loop for a high-fit activated cohort.
- Trigger a reminder from an unfinished valuable task or new collaborative event.
- Intervene before churn when a validated behavioral risk signal appears.
- Repair involuntary churn with failed-payment recovery.
- Test an expansion path tied to demonstrated additional value.
