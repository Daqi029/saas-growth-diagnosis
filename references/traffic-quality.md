# Traffic quality diagnosis

## Diagnose by segment, not blended volume

For each meaningful channel or campaign, compare:

- spend or production cost;
- visits and eligible signups;
- cost per signup and CAC when mature;
- activation rate and time to value;
- trial-to-paid or qualified-pipeline conversion;
- early retention and revenue quality;
- target-customer fit signals such as company size, role, use case, or email domain.

A cheap signup is not good traffic if it does not activate, retain, or pay.

## Symptom → hypothesis → evidence

### Low acquisition cost, low activation, low paid conversion

Likely hypotheses:

- audience targeting is too broad;
- incentive attracts curiosity rather than need;
- content appeals to non-buyers;
- campaign optimizes for a shallow event.

Useful evidence:

- activation and paid conversion by channel, campaign, keyword, and persona;
- user roles, company characteristics, and use cases;
- qualitative review of recent low-intent signups.

### High click-through, low signup, fast exits

Likely hypotheses:

- source promise and landing page do not match;
- users expected a different job or outcome;
- page fails to explain the product quickly;
- mobile or performance problems create friction.

Compare the exact acquisition promise with the first screen. A landing-page issue and a traffic-message issue can coexist; identify which change is upstream.

### One channel is expensive and weak downstream

Compare CAC or cost per signup alongside activation, paid conversion, retention, and customer value. A channel costing twice the average is not automatically bad if its customers retain or spend materially more.

## Directional acquisition-cost context from the source series

- B2B SaaS cost per signup: $50–$200.
- B2C SaaS cost per signup: $10–$50.
- Enterprise SaaS cost per signup: $200–$500.

These figures are not decision rules. Confirm whether the source and user use the same denominator and whether sales cost is included.

## Candidate experiments

Choose experiments that isolate a targeting or promise hypothesis:

- narrow one campaign to a specific job, company size, or competitor-aware audience;
- align one source message and landing-page hero around the same promised outcome;
- add a truthful qualification cue before signup;
- route high-intent and broad-intent cohorts to different pages;
- pause or cap a weak channel only when downstream quality is also weak;
- instrument channel-to-activation and channel-to-paid cohorts before reallocating a large budget.

Avoid advising detailed bid settings, SEO execution, or campaign operations unless separately requested.
