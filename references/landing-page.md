# Landing-page diagnosis

Audit in order: comprehension → trust → action. If the user cannot understand the offer, later proof and CTA polish cannot repair the first failure.

## Layer 1: five-second comprehension

Ask whether a target user can quickly identify:

- what the product does;
- who it is for;
- what concrete outcome or problem it addresses;
- what the product looks like or produces;
- what to do next.

Evidence methods:

- five-second test with five people unfamiliar with the product;
- hero-copy and screenshot inspection;
- average engaged time, bounce or exit behavior, scroll depth, and device segmentation.

Directional signals from the source series:

- 3 of 5 testers cannot explain the product → comprehension problem is supported;
- time under 15 seconds plus bounce above 70% → probable first-screen problem;
- time above 30 seconds with low conversion → continue to trust and action rather than assuming the hero failed.

Do not use analytics thresholds without controlling for traffic intent and page purpose.

Common problems:

- abstract category or vision language;
- no concrete target user or outcome;
- generic “AI-powered platform” positioning;
- visual that decorates rather than demonstrates;
- acquisition promise and hero promise differ.

## Layer 2: thirty-second trust

Check for evidence appropriate to the product's stage:

- concrete social proof;
- quantified outcomes with a verifiable basis;
- customer stories structured as problem → use → result;
- customer logos used with permission;
- security, privacy, refund, cancellation, or “no credit card” reassurance when relevant;
- founder credibility or specific early customer evidence when scale proof is unavailable.

Do not invent user counts, logos, results, testimonials, or certifications. An early product should prefer a specific truthful case over inflated-looking proof.

Directional source signal:

- fewer than two relevant trust elements plus registration-page exits above 50% → trust is a plausible priority;
- three or more relevant trust elements plus registration-page exits below 30% → continue to action-path checks.

## Layer 3: action path

Inspect:

- one visually dominant primary CTA;
- CTA language communicates the next value-bearing action;
- CTA destination matches the promise;
- signup asks only for information needed now;
- mobile interaction, page speed, broken states, and form errors;
- reassurance placed near the risky action;
- unnecessary navigation or competing calls to action.

Do not assume “more prominent CTA” fixes low motivation. Confirm that users understand and trust the offer first.

## Candidate experiments

- Replace an abstract hero with “help [specific user] achieve [specific outcome]” and a real product result.
- Add one truthful case with before/after evidence at the first major doubt point.
- Reduce the primary conversion path to one CTA and remove a nonessential form step.
- Match the hero and CTA to one high-intent acquisition segment.
- Run a five-second test before shipping a full redesign.
